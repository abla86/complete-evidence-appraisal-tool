# Academic Writing & Research Integrity Model

## Purpose

The superprogram can support Master and PhD research writing, but generated prose must remain grounded in project evidence, verified sources, researcher-provided data, and clearly labelled suggestions.

## Source-grounded writing states

- `SOURCE_VERIFIED`: claim is linked to a source whose bibliographic record has been explicitly verified.
- `SOURCE_DETECTED`: source metadata was detected/imported but not independently verified.
- `EVIDENCE_LINKED`: claim is linked to a passage, page, table, figure, dataset, or other evidence location.
- `RESEARCHER_INPUT`: claim or interpretation was supplied by the researcher.
- `AI_SUGGESTION`: wording or reasoning suggestion generated by AI and requiring researcher review.
- `UNSUPPORTED`: no adequate supporting evidence is linked; the system must not present the claim as established fact.

## No-hallucination rules

1. Never invent references, authors, titles, DOI, PMID, quotations, page numbers, datasets, results, statistics, or study findings.
2. Never cite a source unless the reference record exists in the Reference Hub.
3. Never claim that a source supports a statement unless supporting evidence has been linked or the user explicitly accepts the claim as their own interpretation.
4. Never convert detected metadata into verified bibliographic truth automatically.
5. Never fabricate missing information to make a paragraph look complete.
6. When evidence is missing, write a visible gap/verification marker rather than filling the gap with plausible text.
7. Preserve the distinction between source fact, researcher interpretation, synthesis, hypothesis, and AI wording suggestion.
8. Keep an audit trail of source additions, citation changes, evidence links, and major generated-text decisions.
9. AI may propose wording and structure, but the researcher remains responsible for the submitted academic work.
10. AI-generated content must never become the primary cited source for a substantive research claim.

## Retrieval-first writing workflow

`Research question -> search -> source capture -> Reference Hub -> source verification -> evidence extraction -> appraisal -> synthesis -> draft -> citation audit -> reviewer audit -> final export`

A writing request that needs factual support should first retrieve and cite source records from the project library. Pure stylistic editing may operate without retrieval, but must not introduce new factual claims.

## Claim ledger

Every substantive generated paragraph should be traceable through a claim ledger containing:

- claim ID
- text span
- claim type
- source record IDs
- evidence location(s)
- verification state
- confidence/uncertainty note
- researcher approval state

## Academic levels

The same evidence-grounded engine supports:

- Bachelor / advanced undergraduate work
- Master thesis
- Doctoral dissertation / PhD thesis
- Research article
- Systematic review / scoping review
- Protocol
- Conference abstract/poster text
- Research report
- Grant/proposal support

The level changes expected depth, methodological sophistication, argumentation, and output structure; it does not weaken source-integrity rules.

## Master writing workspace

The writer should support:

- introduction/background
- rationale and knowledge gap
- research question/objectives/hypotheses
- theoretical framework
- methodology
- search strategy
- inclusion/exclusion criteria
- critical appraisal
- results
- evidence tables
- analysis/synthesis
- discussion
- strengths/limitations
- ethics
- implications
- conclusion
- abstract
- acknowledgements
- reference list

## PhD writing workspace

In addition to the Master capabilities, support:

- original contribution / novelty statement
- theoretical contribution
- conceptual model
- dissertation architecture
- article-based thesis structure
- study-specific methods
- multi-study integration
- longitudinal or mixed-methods designs
- reproducibility and research-data links
- preregistration/protocol references where relevant
- contribution-to-field mapping
- limitations and boundary conditions
- manuscript/article consistency checks
- publication-ready reference and citation auditing
- thesis-level coherence across chapters/articles

## Research writing modes

- `WRITE_FROM_SOURCES`: draft only from selected source material.
- `SYNTHESIZE_SOURCES`: compare and synthesize multiple sources with citation mapping.
- `WRITE_FROM_PROJECT_DATA`: draft methods/results from researcher-controlled project data.
- `EDIT_ONLY`: improve language without introducing new factual claims.
- `ARGUMENT_AUDIT`: identify unsupported or weak claims without rewriting them as facts.
- `CITATION_AUDIT`: identify claims that lack adequate sources or citations.
- `SUPERVISOR_REVIEW`: critique coherence, methodology, argumentation, and evidence use.
- `EXAMINER_REVIEW`: test the manuscript against configurable assessment criteria.

## Mandatory final checks

Before export the writer should report:

- unsupported substantive claims
- references cited but not used
- claims with missing citations
- citations whose source record is unverified
- quotation/evidence locations that are missing
- references flagged as retracted/changed when such status is known
- contradictions between chapters
- research question/result/conclusion mismatch
- methodological claims not supported by the recorded method
- AI-generated sections that still require researcher review

## Responsible AI disclosure

The application should provide an optional research log documenting AI-assisted drafting, editing, analysis, or retrieval. Institutional, publisher, journal, and course requirements remain controlling.
